# Trademark Policy

**Nooxy** and the Nooxy badge are trademarks of David Raphi.

The [MIT license](LICENSE) grants broad rights to the source code. It does not
grant rights to the name. This page says how the name may be used, so that
people can tell the difference between this project and something built from it.

## You do not need permission to

- Say your site runs on Nooxy, or that your tool works with it.
- Keep the "Made with Nooxy" badge, or turn it off with `showBadge: false`.
- Write about Nooxy, review it, teach it, or compare it to alternatives.
- Publish a plugin, template or guide whose name refers to Nooxy descriptively,
  such as "Astro adapter for Nooxy".
- Fork the repository and keep the name while your changes are still in flight,
  for example on a branch you intend to send back as a pull request.

## Please do not

- Name a fork or a derivative product **Nooxy**, or a name close enough to be
  mistaken for it.
- Publish a package on npm or any other registry using the name Nooxy or a
  variant of it.
- Use the Nooxy badge, or a modified version of it, as the mark for your own
  project.
- Suggest that your product is official, endorsed by, or affiliated with Nooxy
  when it is not.
- Register a domain, social account or company name built around Nooxy in a way
  that implies you run the project.

## Attribution when you fork

The MIT license already requires you to keep the copyright notice and license
text in any substantial portion of the code. Beyond that, a line in your README
saying your project began as a fork of Nooxy is appreciated, and is usually
enough to keep everyone clear on what is what.

## Questions

If you are unsure whether a use is fine, open a
[discussion](https://github.com/draphy/nooxy/discussions) and ask. The intention
here is to stop confusion, not to stop people building things.
